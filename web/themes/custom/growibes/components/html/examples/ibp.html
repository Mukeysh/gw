01 / The challenge

Global information needs local context.

A transparency organisation dealing with country-by-country information has a content problem that is also an information-design problem. A visitor needs to understand a country's status, see the relevant indicators and follow the evidence without getting lost in a database-like experience.

01
Country profiles at scale.

Structured country-level information had to remain consistent enough for comparison while supporting the realities of individual countries.

02
Progress needs a visual language.

Indicators and status information needed to be connected to the profile rather than treated as isolated data points.

03
Evidence must stay connected.

Linked repositories and related resources needed to sit close to the information they supported.

03 / Architecture decisions

Turn complexity into a repeatable pattern.

DECISION 01

Model the country.

A structured profile pattern made country-level content reusable and maintainable instead of creating disconnected bespoke pages.

DECISION 02

Keep evidence adjacent.

Linked repositories and related resources were treated as part of the information journey, helping users move from claim to supporting material.

DECISION 03

Design for an international audience.

Accessibility and multilingual support were built into the experience so global audiences could access the same underlying information system.

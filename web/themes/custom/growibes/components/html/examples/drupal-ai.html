02 / Why Drupal AI

Your best AI advantage may already be inside your content architecture.

Drupal knows more than a page. It can model entities, relationships, taxonomy, workflow state and permissions. That structure is exactly what makes enterprise AI more useful: the system can retrieve context with meaning rather than treating the organization as a pile of documents.

Drupal's 2026 AI roadmap focuses on context management, background agents, design-system integration, content creation/discovery and advanced governance. The project is explicitly moving toward AI that works within real editorial systems.

03 / The trap

The fastest way to create an AI problem is to start with the model.

Enterprise AI projects rarely fail because a model cannot generate text. They fail because nobody decided what the model is allowed to know, where trusted context comes from, what action it can take, who reviews it and how the result is measured.

01

What is the source of truth?

AI needs trusted, structured context — not a random export of the content database.

02

What can it see?

Permissions and audience boundaries have to survive the AI retrieval layer.

03

What can it do?

Answering is easy. Safe actions require explicit workflow boundaries and approvals.

04

How do we know it worked?

Production AI needs evaluation, observability and a way to improve when reality disagrees with the output.

06 / Governance

Intelligence is useful.
Control makes it deployable.

The enterprise buyer is not asking whether AI is impressive. They are asking whether they can defend putting it into production.

CONTEXT
Know what the system is allowed to know.

Define trusted sources, retrieval boundaries and the context available to each workflow.

PERMISSIONS
Respect the access model.

AI should not become a shortcut around the permissions already protecting enterprise content.

HUMAN REVIEW
Keep consequential decisions observable.

Design approval and escalation into the workflow where accuracy or risk demands it.

AUDIT
Know what happened.

Track actions, outputs and changes so teams can evaluate the system instead of trusting a black box.

PORTABILITY
Don't let the model become the architecture.

Keep the application and content boundaries strong enough to evolve as models and providers change.

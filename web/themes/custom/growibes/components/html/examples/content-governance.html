The CMS should not merely store content.
It should understand who is allowed to change it — and when.

Drupal Workflows and Content Moderation support states, transitions, revisions and permissions, allowing organizations to create publishing processes beyond a simple published/unpublished switch.

The real engineering challenge is translating the organization's operating model into a system editors can actually use: clear ownership, safe transitions, useful previews and predictable release behavior.

01 / The friction

Most publishing chaos is a system-design problem.

UNCLEAR OWNERSHIP

Everyone can edit. Nobody owns the outcome.

Roles are too broad, approvals happen in email and the final decision becomes tribal knowledge.

HIDDEN STATES

“Ready” means something different to everyone.

Draft, legal review, translation, scheduled, approved and published need explicit meaning.

RISKY RELEASES

A small content change becomes a production event.

Without previews, revisions and controlled transitions, editors learn to fear the CMS.

02 / The operating model

Make the path to publish visible.

We map the real editorial journey before configuring Drupal. States should represent meaningful business decisions; transitions should represent permissioned actions.

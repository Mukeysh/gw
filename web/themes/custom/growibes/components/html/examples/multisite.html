01 / The real problem

Centralize what should be shared. Decentralize what should not.

Multisite is not automatically the answer to “we have many websites.” The architecture has to reflect organizational structure, content ownership, release cadence, brand relationships and the amount of shared functionality.

Current Drupal guidance explicitly treats multisite as an architectural choice with tradeoffs, while Drupal's enterprise positioning emphasizes centralized governance with distributed control across many brands and markets.

02 / The operating model

Four decisions determine
whether the network scales.

01 / FOUNDATION

What is shared?

Code, components, integrations, security patterns, design tokens and platform services.

02 / OWNERSHIP

Who controls it?

Define what central teams own and what local teams can change without waiting.

03 / DIFFERENCE

What can vary?

Brands and markets need legitimate freedom — content, language, campaigns and experience.

04 / CHANGE

How does it ship?

Shared releases need predictable testing, deployment and rollback without turning every site into a hostage.

03 / Governance without bottlenecks

Control the platform.
Don't control every decision.

The strongest enterprise networks create a clear boundary between platform standards and local autonomy. Central teams protect the system; local teams operate within it.

04 / Where complexity hides

The hard part isn't launching site number 20.

The hard part is making site number 20 feel independent while keeping the platform underneath coherent.

DESIGN

One system, many expressions.

Shared components and design foundations create consistency without forcing every brand into the same visual identity.

CONTENT

Reuse without duplication.

Structure content so teams can reuse what is genuinely common while keeping local content ownership clear.

DELIVERY

Release without fear.

Automate the boring parts of provisioning, testing and deployment so network growth does not multiply operational risk.

05 / Architecture choices

There is no single
“multisite architecture.”

SHARED CORE

One codebase, multiple sites

Useful when sites genuinely share modules, themes and release patterns, with separate content and configuration where required.

PLATFORM

Shared services, independent sites

Useful when brands need more autonomy while still consuming common design systems, APIs or platform services.

FEDERATED

Different systems, common standards

Useful when acquisitions, business units or technology constraints make a single Drupal runtime unrealistic.

DECISION

Choose based on organizational reality

The right architecture follows ownership, change frequency, technical capacity and business relationships — not fashion.

06 / AI-ready platform operations

Make the network easier to operate as it gets smarter.

Drupal's 2026 strategy puts AI into the workflow layer and emphasizes agency-friendly, repeatable implementation. For a multi-site enterprise, that makes standardized architecture even more valuable: AI-assisted tasks are most useful when the underlying sites, models, workflows and governance are predictable.

The goal is not to automate every decision. It is to reduce repetitive platform work while keeping humans accountable for high-impact changes.

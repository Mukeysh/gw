01 / The problem

Personalization fails when it becomes a pile of exceptions.

Enterprise personalization is not simply showing a different banner to a different visitor. It is the operating model connecting audience definition, content, data, rules, experimentation, privacy and measurement.

The Drupal ecosystem supports audience targeting through behavior, taxonomy, geography and external decision engines. The engineering challenge is making those signals work together while keeping authors, marketers and technical teams in control.

02 / The experience system

Five layers.
One experience.

01 / AUDIENCE

Define who matters.

Segments can combine known audience attributes, behavior, geography, account context and business intent.

02 / CONTENT

Make variation intentional.

Build reusable content variants that editors can understand, govern and preview rather than hard-coding exceptions.

03 / DECISION

Keep the rules visible.

Use explicit conditions and decision logic so teams can inspect why an experience changed.

04 / DELIVERY

Respect the architecture.

Personalization must coexist with caching, performance, headless delivery and the rest of the platform.

05 / MEASUREMENT

Prove the difference.

Connect experience changes to engagement and conversion outcomes instead of celebrating personalization as an end in itself.

03 / Personalization without chaos

More relevance.
Less magic.

Good personalization should be explainable. Teams should know what audience was targeted, what content changed, what rule fired and what result followed.

That matters even more as AI enters the experience layer. Drupal's current strategy explicitly points toward AI-operated workflows while keeping humans in the loop — making governance part of the experience architecture, not an afterthought.

04 / Optimization

Don't personalize blindly.
Run the experiment.

Personalization and experimentation belong in the same operating loop. Start with a hypothesis, define the audience, ship a controlled experience, measure the outcome and keep what earns its place.

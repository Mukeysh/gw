02 / The real job

Migration is a chance to remove the past without losing its value.

Years of content, taxonomy, media, redirects, permissions, forms, custom code and editorial habits are hiding inside the old platform. Some of it is essential. Some of it is accidental. Some of it should never be migrated.

Drupal's own migration guidance emphasizes source inventory, clean destination environments, mapping, staged migration and testing rather than treating an upgrade as a simple version change.

03 / Why migrations fail

The expensive mistake is moving everything.

Migration projects go wrong when teams optimize for “data moved” instead of “business preserved.” The source system contains years of decisions — and years of clutter.

01

Content is migrated before it is understood.

Duplicate, obsolete and structurally inconsistent content becomes permanent debt in the new system.

02

Customizations are treated as features.

Some need rebuilding. Some can disappear. Some reveal business rules nobody documented.

03

SEO is treated as a redirect spreadsheet.

URLs, metadata, internal links and search equity need to be part of the migration architecture.

04

The cutover becomes the first real test.

That is too late. Rehearsals, validation and incremental approaches reduce the unknowns before launch.

06 / Choose your transition

There is no universal
cutover.

01 / CONTROLLED CUTOVER

Big bang.

Appropriate when the estate is manageable, the destination is well rehearsed and a controlled maintenance window is acceptable.

02 / CONTINUITY

Incremental.

Useful when the existing platform must remain active while sections, content or capabilities move progressively into the new system.

03 / COMPLEX ECOSYSTEM

Hybrid.

When legacy systems, APIs and regulated workflows cannot move together, isolate the boundaries and migrate the platform in controlled stages.

07 / Evidence

The details are where confidence comes from.

Real enterprise migrations demonstrate why content mapping, custom behavior, performance, integration and cutover strategy have to be engineered together.

REALITY
10,000+ pages can still hide a systems problem.

A recent enterprise Drupal migration case involved 10,000+ pages, 500+ custom forms, external dashboards and separate databases — ultimately consolidating them into a unified Drupal architecture.

METHOD
Incremental migration is a real engineering pattern.

Drupal documents staged approaches where the source stays live while content is migrated into staging and production in controlled passes.

DISCIPLINE
Inventory first. Migration second.

Drupal's preparation guidance explicitly recommends understanding the source modules, dependencies and destination prerequisites before execution.

OUTCOME
The goal is not “migration complete.”

The goal is a platform that is easier to operate, safer to change and structurally ready for what the business needs next.
